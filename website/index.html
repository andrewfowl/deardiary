Privacy

A diary is private.
This one actually is.

No cloud, no account, no telemetry

Screenshots, timeline, and journal live in one folder on your PC. There is no DearDiary server to trust, because there isn't one at all.

Consent before capture

Nothing is recorded until you finish setup and press "Start recording." Pause any time from the tray; it also stops on lock and idle.

Screenshots burn after reading

Raw images are deleted after 7 days (your call, 1–90). Only the written journal — text you can read and audit — is kept.

You choose the AI — or none at all

Gemini, Claude, any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, or Ollama running locally, in which case nothing ever leaves your machine.

The one honest caveat

Unless you use a local model, screenshots are sent to the AI provider you configured, under your key and their API terms. That's the whole data story — we'd rather you read it here than in a settings tooltip.

Questions

Asked before trusting an app
with your screen

Where does my data go?

Nowhere, by default. Screenshots and the journal database are stored in your Windows user profile. The only network traffic is to the AI provider you configure — and with Ollama running locally, there is none at all.

Do I need to pay for an AI provider too?

Usually not. Google Gemini's free tier comfortably covers typical daily use, and Ollama is free. Prefer Claude or OpenAI? Bring your own key and pay them directly — typically cents per day.

What happens when my trial or annual license ends?

Recording and analysis pause — that's all. Every day you already recorded stays fully browsable, summarizable history. A diary that confiscates itself wouldn't be much of a diary.

Can I pause it for sensitive work?

One click on the tray icon. Capture also stops automatically when your screen locks or you go idle.

Lifetime vs annual — what's the difference?

Features are identical. Lifetime is one $49 payment that never expires; annual is $19/year and works while active. Both cover two of your own PCs.

Is there a Mac version?

DearDiary is Windows-first. On macOS, we'll happily point you to Dayflow — the open-source app that inspired us.

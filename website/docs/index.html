Getting started

From install to your first AI-written timeline in about 20 minutes — most of which is DearDiary quietly doing its job.

Requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit)
  • An AI provider: a free Google Gemini key is the fastest start; Ollama works fully offline

Install

  1. Download the installer from the releases page.
  2. Run it. DearDiary starts automatically and opens the setup wizard.

First-run setup

Nothing is recorded until you finish the three-step wizard:

  1. Welcome & privacy — what DearDiary records and where it's stored (spoiler: only on your PC).
  2. Choose your AI — pick a provider, paste its key, and click Test connection. You can skip this and do it later in Settings, but no timeline is generated until a provider works.
  3. How recording works — capture cadence and tray behavior. Click Start recording and you're done.

Your first timeline

DearDiary takes a screenshot of your primary display every 30 seconds while you're active, and analyzes them in ~15-minute batches. So:

  • The status footer (bottom-left) shows Recording and a count of screenshots pending analysis.
  • After about 15 minutes the first timeline cards appear. Impatient? Click Analyze now in the status footer.

The five views

ViewWhat it's for
TimelineYour day as cards — what, when, how long, categorized, distractions flagged.
JournalFocus stats, category breakdown, and a one-click AI-written entry for the day.
Ask your dayChat over the selected day's timeline: "What was I doing at 3pm?"
StandupYesterday / Today / Blockers drafted from your actual activity. Copy or polish with AI.
SettingsProvider, recording cadence, retention, and your license.

Living in the tray

  • Closing the window keeps DearDiary recording in the system tray.
  • Right-click the tray icon to pause/resume recording or quit entirely.
  • Capture pauses automatically when your screen locks or you go idle for 3 minutes (configurable).
Tip: give it a normal workday before judging the output. The timeline gets dramatically more useful once there's a full day to summarize, ask about, and turn into a standup.
